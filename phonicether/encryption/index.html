PhonicEther Encryption Specification

Protocol version 3. Published 5 November 2026.

Summary

  • PhonicEther encrypts audio and track information sent between a Mac and the iPhones and iPads paired with it.
  • This page publishes the complete format. Security depends on secret keys, not on keeping the format secret.
  • All algorithms are public standards. The packet formats and their combination are specific to PhonicEther and are described here.
  • The app does not use encryption as a general service. A user cannot change the algorithms, keys or formats.

2. Notation

  • a || b means a followed by b. Integers are big-endian.
  • HKDF(ikm, salt, info, n) returns n bytes. An empty salt means zero bytes.
  • CTR(key, iv, data) is AES-256-CTR. HMAC(key, data) is HMAC-SHA-256.
  • IV(nonce) is a 12-byte nonce followed by four zero bytes.
  • Public keys are 65-byte uncompressed X9.63 P-256 points. Text labels are UTF-8 with no terminator.

4. Pairing with a QR code

  1. The QR code carries the Mac public key, a session identifier and a per-window proof secret.
  2. The device creates an ephemeral P-256 key. It proves that it scanned the code with HMAC(secret, "sa-pair-v3-identity" || devicePublic || receiverIdentityPublic || "|v=" || version || "|id=" || deviceID).
  3. The Mac and the device compute Z with ECDH, then TK = HKDF(Z, empty, "phonicether-provisioning-v1", 32).
  4. The Mac replies with macPublic (65) || nonce (12) || CTR(TK, IV(nonce), groupKey) (32) || HMAC(TK, macPublic || nonce || ciphertext) (32). The device rejects a Mac public key that differs from the QR code.

5. Pairing with a guest PIN

  1. Both sides derive w0 and w1 from the guest PIN with PBKDF2, using the salt "sa-guest-v1". Each value is 40 bytes reduced modulo the P-256 group order.
  2. They run SPAKE2+ with the context "PhonicEther-guest-v1|device-id=" || deviceID || "|phone-ephemeral=" || devicePublic || "|receiver-identity=" || receiverIdentityPublic. The PIN does not cross the network.
  3. The transport key is HKDF(len(Kshared) || Kshared || len(Z) || Z, empty, "phonicether-guest-identity-v3" || len(context) || context, 32), where Z comes from ECDH.
  4. The Mac sends the group key in the same layout as section 4. The tag covers a fixed label and the length-prefixed context, Mac public key, nonce and ciphertext.

6. Session announcement

The Mac gives each device the media key in a text message: "SA3:" followed by the Base64 of this packet.

The receiver verifies the tag before it decrypts, and drops the packet if the tag fails. The epoch and counter keep each IV unique for a media key. If no key is available, the Mac does not send the audio.

A large message can be split into fragments. Each fragment starts with its own header and ends with the first 16 bytes of HMAC(mediaKey, "sa-media-fragment-v1" || fragment).

8. Track information

Each device receives its own copy. For each type of message:

material = HKDF(deviceControlKey, session || epoch (8), info, 64). The first 32 bytes encrypt and the last 32 bytes authenticate. The IV is 16 random bytes.

MessageInfo labelLayout
Title, artist and albumphonicether.source-metadata.v1"SAM1" || epoch (8) || revision (8) || IV (16) || ciphertext of JSON || tag (16)
Artwork fragmentphonicether.source-artwork.v1"SAA1" || epoch (8) || revision (8) || HMAC(authKey, SHA-256(image)) (32) || index (2) || count (2) || total (4) || IV (16) || ciphertext of SHA-256(image) and an image fragment || tag (16)

The tag is the first 16 bytes of HMAC(authKey, all earlier bytes).

9. Control messages

Control messages are authenticated, not encrypted. They contain no audio and no track text.

  • Reports: payload || unixTime (8) || tag (16), with the tag from HMAC(deviceControlKey, "sa-media-report-v3" || payload || unixTime).
  • Leases: a text body with HMAC(deviceControlKey, "SALEASE:v3:" || body).

Discovery messages are not encrypted. They can show the Mac's name and network address, and they contain no audio.

10. Changes

Each new media epoch gets a new random media key, and a removed device does not receive it. We will update this page when the format changes.

11. Export classification

The developer self-classified PhonicEther on 5 November 2026 as a mass market encryption item, ECCN 5D992.c, under 15 CFR 740.17(b)(1) and Note 3 to Category 5, Part 2 of the US Export Administration Regulations. This is the developer's own classification, not a government determination.
